About Me

Hello! I'm M. Waseem Ashraf, a PhD student in Mechanical Engineering at Brigham Young University, working under Dr. Eric R. Homer. My research focuses on shape memory alloys, phase transformations, and the coupling between mechanical behavior and microstructural evolution. I use computational methods such as finite element modeling, crystal plasticity, and machine learning to better understand and predict material behavior.

Outside the lab, I love exploring the outdoors—whether it’s hiking trails, mountain biking through Utah’s canyons, or playing basketball with friends. These activities keep me energized and ready for new challenges.

Background

I’ve been a maker for as long as I can remember. Back in 7th grade, I joined my school’s aeromodelling club and built my first RC plane out of balsa wood. By the time I finished high school, I had designed and flown at least seven planes, each one teaching me something new about aerodynamics, materials, and design.

Presenting my RC airplane during Parents Day (9th grade)

That early start grew into a career in engineering. During my undergraduate studies at the Pakistan Institute of Engineering and Applied Sciences, I worked on ambitious projects like designing an air ambulance using aerodynamic simulations and leading a Hyperloop Pod Competition team developing passive magnetic levitation systems. Later, I expanded my skills through teaching and freelance design, creating solutions ranging from MEMS devices and microfluidic systems to aerospace components and medical device prototypes.

Now, as a PhD candidate at BYU, I study the mechanical and electrical properties (including dielectric tensor) of advanced ceramics through NSF-sponsored research. Alongside research, I also serve as President of the Mechanical Engineering Graduate Association, helping foster collaboration and community among graduate students.
