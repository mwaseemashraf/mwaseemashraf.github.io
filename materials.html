Thermodynamics · First‑Principles

Quasi‑Harmonic Free Energies and Phase‑Transformation Enthalpy in Zirconia

I computed temperature‑ and pressure‑dependent stability of zirconia polymorphs using DFPT phonons and the quasi‑harmonic approximation (QHA). The workflow integrates static DFT energies, phonon density of states, and volume‑dependent vibrational free energies to obtain G(T,P) and the enthalpy change across martensitic t↔m and c↔t boundaries.

  • Generated converged E(V) and phonon spectra with VASP + Phonopy; fit Birch‑Murnaghan equations of state for each phase.
  • Evaluated ΔHtrans(T) and ΔStrans(T); derived dP/dT (Clapeyron slope) to compare with experimental trends.
  • Assessed soft‑mode behavior and anharmonic sensitivity to strain, linking phonon instabilities to the ferroelastic transformation pathway.
  • The ab initio results had 0.7 coorelation with experimental phase change enthalpies. We had some doubts about the applicability of QHAem approximation.

Electro‑Mechanical Coupling

Orientation Effects in Electrically Induced Phase Transformation of Zirconia

This study probes how crystallographic orientation affects E‑field‑induced t→m transformation in zirconia. We analyze variant selection, orientation-dependent E-filed, and orientation-dependent susceptibility mismatch to figure out most optimal directions for E-field and resulting variants.

  • Used a Clausius-Clapeyron framework with electromechanical coupling to rationalize orientation‑dependent required E-field.
  • Mapped variant selection to predict child phase orientation.
  • We found out that for the most optimal E-field directions we always get B-correspondence .
  • Compared single vs poly crystals zirconia for actuation suitability.

Dielectrics · Defects

First‑Principles Dielectric Response of Y/Ce‑Doped Zirconia

I performed DFPT calculations to separate electronic and ionic contributions to the static permittivity in zirconia doped with Y2O3 and CeO2. The focus is how oxygen‑vacancy configurations, local symmetry breaking, and phonon mode effective charges (Born charge tensors) modulate the low‑frequency dielectric constant relevant for sensing and energy‑storage applications.

  • Constructed defect‑containing supercells with realistic vacancy‑dopant pairing; compared site‑selective vs random vacancy models.
  • Analyzed LO‑TO splittings, mode‑resolved dielectric activity, and phonon softening to quantify the ionic contribution.
  • Benchmarked Vegard‑like trends in lattice parameters against composition and linked them to changes in polarizability.
  • Provided guidance on dopant level/orientation that maximizes ε0 while retaining mechanical robustness.

Microscopy · Radiation Effects

Electron‑Beam Stability of Metal Fluoride Thin Films (SEM/EDS/EBSD)

Using scanning electron microscopy with EDS and EBSD, I examined the beam‑induced evolution of alkali/alkaline‑earth metal fluoride films. The work distinguishes between radiolysis‑driven mass loss, chemical reduction, and charging‑mediated microcracking, establishing dose‑rate thresholds and imaging protocols that preserve crystallographic information.

  • Quantified stoichiometry drift under controlled beam current and dwell time; monitored F‑loss signatures via EDS.
  • Correlated EBSD pattern quality with cumulative dose to set safe operating windows for phase indexing.
  • Recommended low‑kV imaging strategies and conductive capping layers to mitigate charging and damage.
  • Delivered a practical SEM workflow for sensitive ionic compounds used in optics and solid‑state devices.

Functional Composites

Plasticity & Microstructure in Composite Nanomaterial Strain Sensors

I characterized plastic deformation and microstructural evolution in conductive nanocomposite strain sensors to improve gauge factor, linearity, and durability in biomechanical environments. The analysis connects filler network topology and matrix yielding to the electromechanical response and hysteresis.

  • Related percolation pathways and tunneling distances to resistance change under cyclic strain.
  • Used micromechanics to separate elastic, plastic, and viscoelastic contributions to signal drift.
  • Outlined processing knobs (surface functionalization, dispersion, cure schedules) that stabilize the conductive network.
  • Proposed encapsulation and patterning strategies for repeatable response in fluid‑exposed settings.
