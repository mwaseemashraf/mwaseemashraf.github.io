FEA and Thermal Modeling of Human Head Exposure to Mobile Phones

Developed a four-layer human-head model to simulate the Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) and temperature rise when exposed to HF electromagnetic radiation from mobile devices. Published in Proceedings of SPIE (Optical Interactions with Tissue and Cells XXXII).

  • Modeled distinct tissue layers—skin, fat, bone, and brain—with realistic dielectric and thermal properties.
  • Computed electromagnetic absorption (SAR) distribution and localized heating using FEA-based thermal analysis.
  • Assessed exposure safety by correlating SAR values with thermal response to guide safer device designs.

Microfluidic Cancer Cell Counter Based on the Coulter Principle

Developed and optimized a microfluidic device leveraging the Coulter Principle to count and characterize cells. Used COMSOL Multiphysics to simulate fluid dynamics and improve measurement precision and reliability. The device was designed to detect cancer cells through their electrophoretic behavior.

  • Simulated fluid flow and particle trajectories to refine channel geometry and electrode placement.
  • Integrated electrophoresis-based detection to differentiate cancer cells from normal cells.
  • Improved sensitivity and robustness of the system through iterative design and multiphysics modeling.
